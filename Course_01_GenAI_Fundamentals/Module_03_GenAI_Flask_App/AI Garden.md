# AI Academy: Growing Your AI Garden

**Speaker:** Nicholas Renotte, Chief AI Engineer at IBM Client Engineering

## Introduction
> "You can kind of think of an AI model as a vegetable you want to grow in your garden."

*   Before planting, you must research the weather and water requirements; otherwise, it might die before it flowers. 
*   As it grows, you need to evaluate and re-optimize the care provided so it can thrive and bloom. 
*   For an entire garden, you need a variety of vegetables (a multi-model approach) that interact safely, because you cannot survive on carrots alone.

---

## The Multi-Model Approach
If you want your AI garden to grow, ensure you are using a variety of models. This multi-model approach allows you to pick and choose the right model for the right use case. 

When evaluating a model to find the right fit, ask specific, important questions:
*   Who built it?
*   What data was it trained on?
*   What guardrails are in place?
*   What risks and regulations need to be considered and accounted for?

---

## Steps to Choosing and Implementing the Right Model

### 1. Start with a Prompt
The process begins with a prompt—a textual input or instruction that goes into a large language model to set up the basics of the AI. 
*   A good prompt clearly articulates your use case, the user problem, the ask of the technology, and the guardrails for what "good" looks like.

### 2. Research Available Models
Look at key factors such as:
*   Model size
*   Performance
*   Costs
*   Risks
*   Deployment methods

### 3. Evaluate and Test
*   Use the information collected to evaluate the models against your prompt.
*   **Start Large, Then Go Small:** Begin with a large model until it satisfies your original prompt, then try to duplicate that result using smaller models.
*   Experiment by passing the same prompt through different models to see which works best.

### 4. Continuous Evaluation and Governance
Choosing the best model is not the end of the process. You must tend to your AI garden:
*   Continually evaluate and govern the model with ongoing testing against performance and cost benchmarks.
*   Update the data and the prompt as needed to keep them relevant.
*   Test new models as they become available to avoid vendor lock-in as internal and external situations change.

---

## Implementation and Team Collaboration
Implementing an AI model requires a team that crosses disciplines and lines of business. 
*   Treat it as a distinctly collaborative project that takes multiple teams to get up and running, rather than treating it as proprietary to any one department.
*   Ensure the team can diagnose performance benchmarks, measure unique metrics, and produce datasets showing how everything is calculated. 
*   Continuous testing, governance, and optimization are essential to keep models up to date.

*Models evolve, so your strategy and choices need to grow towards the sun instead of withering on the vine.*
